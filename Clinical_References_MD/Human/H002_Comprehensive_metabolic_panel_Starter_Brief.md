# H002: Comprehensive metabolic panel

- **Domain**: Human
- **Species Scope**: Human
- **Clinical Category**: Clinical chemistry
- **Reference Level**: Starter Clinical Brief
- **Source Verification Date**: 2026-11-05
- **Editorial Status**: Source-checked educational draft. No independent clinical review.

**Common Aliases**: CMP, Chem 14, Chemistry panel, Metabolic panel

## Plain-Language Overview

A CMP is a collection of chemistry tests performed on one blood sample. It gives a broad view of several body functions, while any particular abnormal result may need a more specific investigation.

## Clinical Purpose

Measures 14 blood substances to provide information about kidney and liver health, glucose, proteins, electrolytes, and acid-base balance. It may support investigation or treatment monitoring.

## How the Test Works

Different components use different analytical reactions. In Mayo's documented serum panel, sodium, potassium, and chloride are measured using ion-selective electrodes, which translate ion concentration differences into electrical signals. Other components use photometry, measuring changes in light absorption during chemical or enzymatic reactions. These are examples of one laboratory's methods, not required methods for every CMP.

## Specimen Collection & Procedure

A health professional draws venous blood. The panel includes glucose, calcium, electrolytes, albumin, total protein, liver-related enzymes, bilirubin, urea nitrogen, and creatinine.

## Patient / Animal Preparation

Fasting may be requested. Follow the ordering service's instructions and report medicines and supplements.

## Interpreting Results

Results are interpreted together with the person's history and treatment. An abnormal component may prompt targeted follow-up testing.

## Detailed Clinical Interpretation

Assess patterns across components rather than assigning a diagnosis from a single flag. Some reports add calculations such as the anion gap, derived from sodium, chloride, and bicarbonate, or an estimated glomerular filtration rate, an estimate of kidney filtering based on creatinine. These calculated results should be identified separately from directly measured analytes.

## Limitations & Confounders

The panel provides clues rather than a complete diagnosis. Medicines and other circumstances can affect its components.

## Risks & Pre-Analytical Considerations

The blood draw can cause brief discomfort or bruising.

## Species & Population Notes

The named human CMP should not be assumed equivalent to an animal chemistry panel; panel composition and interpretation must be documented separately.

## Professional & Technical Notes

Record the individual analytes, units, and laboratory reference intervals. A basic metabolic panel contains fewer components and is a separate panel. Document fasting status, specimen type, and sample quality. Hemolysis means red cells have broken down in the sample; Mayo rejects grossly hemolyzed specimens for this panel. Use the performing laboratory's own collection, handling, and rejection criteria.

## Related Diagnostic Tests

A urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio provides complementary information about kidney damage. Targeted repeat chemistry or other tests depend on the clinical question. A CMP and a basic metabolic panel should be cataloged separately because they include different components.
