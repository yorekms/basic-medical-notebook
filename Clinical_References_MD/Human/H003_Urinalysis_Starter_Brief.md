# H003: Urinalysis

- **Domain**: Human
- **Species Scope**: Human
- **Clinical Category**: Urine testing
- **Reference Level**: Starter Clinical Brief
- **Source Verification Date**: 2026-11-05
- **Editorial Status**: Source-checked educational draft. No independent clinical review.

**Common Aliases**: UA, Urine analysis, Routine urine test

## Plain-Language Overview

Urinalysis looks at a urine sample for chemical changes and material such as blood cells or crystals. It offers clues about urinary and other health problems; several different conditions can produce similar findings.

## Clinical Purpose

Examines urine for clues to urinary tract and kidney problems, diabetes, and other conditions. It can also help monitor an existing condition or treatment.

## How the Test Works

Chemical testing commonly uses a reagent strip with pads that react to substances in urine. Microscopy examines the urine sediment for cells, crystals, and casts. A cast is a molded structure arising within kidney tubules. Components depend on the order and laboratory workflow.

## Specimen Collection & Procedure

A urine sample is assessed for appearance and chemical findings; examination may also identify cells, crystals, casts, or microorganisms. Follow the requested collection method.

## Patient / Animal Preparation

Collection may use a clean-catch sample. Follow container and return instructions. Report medicines and menstrual or other external bleeding; change medicines only if instructed.

## Interpreting Results

Findings may include blood, protein, glucose, ketones, acidity, and microscopic material. The meaning depends on the combination of findings and the clinical situation.

## Detailed Clinical Interpretation

Check sample quality before interpreting the pattern. Labcorp documents that vitamin C can interfere with some strip reactions and that storage conditions affect white cell survival. Crystals may appear during storage, so a crystal finding must be related to specimen freshness and the clinical question. These are method-dependent limitations rather than reasons to dismiss every abnormal result.

## Limitations & Confounders

Skin germs or external blood can contaminate a sample. A finding such as blood has several possible causes and may need additional tests.

## Risks & Pre-Analytical Considerations

Ordinary urine collection has no known direct test risk. This entry does not cover invasive collection methods.

## Species & Population Notes

Human collection instructions and interpretation do not establish animal collection methods or reference intervals.

## Professional & Technical Notes

Record the collection method and which examination components were performed. Retain the laboratory's units and applicable interpretation guidance. Retain collection and receipt times, storage conditions, and whether microscopy or culture was reflexed. Follow the local laboratory's transport instructions, because requirements can differ between laboratories.

## Related Diagnostic Tests

A urine culture investigates bacterial growth and is a distinct test, even when performed through a reflex pathway triggered by urinalysis findings. A urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio quantifies albumin relative to creatinine and complements kidney assessment; it is not interchangeable with the whole urinalysis.
